Ingredient-label OCR guide

How to scan an ingredient label more clearly

Small type, reflective wrappers, curved containers, and uneven lighting can all change what OCR recognizes. A better photo makes the result easier to review.

Before taking the photo

  1. Find the complete ingredient panel. Include the beginning and end of the printed list rather than a front-of-package claim.
  2. Reduce glare. Move the package or light source until bright reflections no longer cover letters.
  3. Flatten the surface when practical. Curved cans, folded bags, and wrinkled wrappers can hide or distort words.
  4. Fill the frame with text. Keep the camera parallel to the label and hold it steady until the type looks sharp.
  5. Check the result against the package. Pay particular attention to punctuation, nested ingredients, and words split across lines.

Common OCR trouble spots

  • Low contrast between type and packaging
  • Text printed across a seam or sharp curve
  • Multiple languages packed into one panel
  • Decorative fonts, very small type, or damaged labels
  • Shadows cast by the phone or the photographer
Always keep the package in the loop. OCR and ingredient reference data may contain errors. Use the recognized text as a reading aid, then verify it against the complete current label.
